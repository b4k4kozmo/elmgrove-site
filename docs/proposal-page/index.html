Where we are

Same site since April 2024

Elmgrove Games! We are a group of indie game devs out of Seattle, WA Games · Contact · Itch Today: three pages, nine games on one long list

What holds it back

  • Each game gets one paragraph and an itch link.
  • Links wait 1.5 seconds before they go anywhere, and the back button and middle-click don't work.
  • Music tries to start by itself. Browsers block it, and visitors find it jarring.
  • No phone layout. Search engines and link previews see almost nothing.
  • The security certificate belongs to the host, not to us, so browsers warn people away.

What we keep

  • The tree in sunglasses. The Gold Box pixel font. The portal click. The Gobblin' tune.

The move, step 1

The whole old site is saved in our new GitHub repo (elmgrove-site). GitHub Pages is set up for elmgrove.games, which means free hosting, a free certificate, and nothing to renew but the domain. Email becomes simple forwarding: cosmo@, kyle@, krasiv@, robert@, team@ (and contact@) land in our own inboxes. Cosmo does four steps in Namecheap; the hosting stays until everything is checked.

The idea

Climb into the tree

You arrive at the elm from the logo, at dusk. Inside the trunk is a bunker of a treehouse: wood-panelled, warm, a little scrappy, with a row of arcade cabinets, one per game. Loot n' Goblin is our biggest hit, so the place is a bit goblin-y. Its loot is green emeralds, and its cover has the same purple-to-green sky as our logo. The new game stays a secret; at most, "something is digging under the roots".

Our old pixel font stays for headings and cabinet marquees. Running text uses a clear, easy-to-read face so the pages stay comfortable on phones.

Fun, never in the way

Every link is a real link and opens straight away. Sound stays off until you turn it on. Little things to find (emeralds hidden around the site) but nothing you have to do.

The arcade

Our games, from the Elmgrove itch

Up front, a mix of the most successful and the most recent. The rest go on a shelf in the back room. Every game gets its own page: art, screenshots, how it plays, who made it, and buttons to play in the browser or download from itch.
